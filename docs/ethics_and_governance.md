# Ethics, Privacy, and Data Governance

## Data characteristics

The project is designed primarily around secondary public data. Aggregated mortality, population, and infrastructure datasets generally present lower privacy risk than individual records, but small cells, rare causes, detailed geography, or linked datasets can increase disclosure risk.

## Governance principles

- data minimization;
- purpose limitation;
- provenance and licensing documentation;
- secure storage of restricted files;
- separation of raw, processed, and public-release data;
- review of disclosure risk before publication;
- compliance with institutional research requirements and Brazilian law.

## Personal data

No directly identifiable personal data should be stored in the public repository. If individual-level or restricted data are later incorporated, they must be held in an approved secure environment and replaced in the public repository by synthetic, aggregated, or metadata-only artifacts.

## Brazilian data protection context

Processing activities involving personal data must be assessed under the Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais (LGPD) and relevant institutional rules. Public availability of a dataset does not eliminate the need for responsible processing, transparency, and risk assessment.

## Ethical review

The need for ethics committee review or exemption must be confirmed with the responsible institution based on the final datasets, linkage strategy, level of aggregation, and intended dissemination. This document does not replace a formal institutional determination.

## Responsible GeoAI

GeoAI and machine-learning outputs should be evaluated for territorial bias, uneven data quality, explainability, reproducibility, and the risk of stigmatizing communities. Maps and rankings must be contextualized and should avoid implying that vulnerable territories or residents are responsible for structural health outcomes.
